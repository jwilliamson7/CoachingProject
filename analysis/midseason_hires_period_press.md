# Midseason hires: interim or permanent? Evidence from period press

Compiled 2026-10-28. Covers three midseason NFL head-coach appointments: Coryell (SD 1978), Berry (NE 1984) and Levy (BUF 1986).

## How to read this file

- **Contemporaneous** means published at the time of the hire or within that season (the end of 1984 or 1986, etc.). **Retrospective** means obituaries, team "history" pages, books and later features. Retrospective sources are listed only as leads or context. They are not evidence of status at hire.
- **Read status:** "full text" means the page was fetched and its content was examined. "Snippet only" means the page could not be fetched (paywall, 403 or blocked domain), and what is recorded comes from the search engine's index of that page.
- **Quote fidelity caveat:** WebFetch returns page content through a summarizing model, which reproduces quotes of up to about 125 characters on request. The quotes marked "full text" below were requested word-for-word, but they should be spot-checked against the URL before they are cited in print. Snippet-only material is paraphrase-grade. It must be verified against the original before it is quoted.
- **Archives that could not be reached from this environment:** nytimes.com, apnews.com, latimes.com and chicagotribune.com (domains blocked for the tool); washingtonpost.com/archive (HTTP 403); newspapers.com clip and page views (HTTP 403, though the search engine indexes their OCR text); cdnc.ucr.edu (403); buffalonews.com (TollBit paywall); vault.si.com and si.com/vault (HTTP 404 to the fetcher, though the search index has their text); web.archive.org (blocked); Google Books API (HTTP 429); forbes.com and boltsfromtheblue.com (403). This is why most contract details could not be read directly. **Recommended follow-up (manual, in a browser):** the NYT TimesMachine stories for 1978-09-26, 1984-10-26 and 1986-11-04, and the newspapers.com clips listed below.
- **Definitional trap that explains the Wikipedia inconsistency:** retrospective reference works often label every midseason replacement "interim" by timing alone, whatever the contract. Example: PFRA's *Coffin Corner* (2004) defines the term as follows: "The interim coach is a top assistant, a front office person, or even an unemployed head coach who has the unenviable task of taking over a team in mid-season." It then lists Coryell, Berry and Levy under that heading (full text read, see below). A Wikipedia infobox reading "(interim)" may reflect this timing-based usage, not the terms of the appointment.

**What the snippets show**

- C1, subhead (snippet): "Coryell Replaces Prothro As Coach of 1-3 Chargers." Lede (snippet, truncated): "Tommy Prothro, who had high hopes for the San Diego Chargers this season, resigned as the National Football League team's head coach yesterday after ..."
- C1, Prothro quote (snippet): "Last winter I told (Charger owner) Gene Kleim [sic] I would coach only one more year because I felt we had a good team and would have a good year," said the 58-year-old Prothro.
- C2 (snippet, paraphrase-grade): the column calls the Cardinals' "dealing coach Don Coryell to the San Diego Chargers for a No. 3 draft choice" the worst trade the Cardinals ever made. Lede fragment: "Had he been seeking revenge, Coryell could not have picked a more opportune time to have his Chargers shut out defending conference champion Denver, 23-0 ..."
- C5 (snippet, paraphrase-grade): Coryell had "never coached a defense" this good "until he took over as head coach of the San Diego Chargers" that week. "Tommy Prothro, who resigned Monday, had wanted to quit last winter." Owner Gene Klein "had had his eye on Coryell right along" but had promised not to replace Prothro unless Prothro asked.
- **Interim wording:** none of the snippets seen uses "interim", "acting" or "for the remainder of the season". Every one calls Coryell the "head coach" or says he was "hired" or "replaces" Prothro.
- **Contract terms at hire:** NOT FOUND. No length or expiry year was visible in any snippet.
- **Compensation to the Cardinals (strong structural evidence):** Coryell was still bound to St. Louis, and the Chargers gave the Cardinals a 3rd-round draft pick for his rights (C2: "for a No. 3 draft choice"). Sources disagree on the draft year: Wikipedia says 1980, and the ultimate70s.com transaction database says "San Diego's #3 pick in the 1981 draft ... as compensation for hiring head coach Don Coryell". That database (https://www.ultimate70s.com/seventies_history/19780925/football) is a compilation, not a 1978 publication.
- **End-of-season coaching search or new contract:** no evidence of a post-1978 search or of a separate retention contract was found.

### Retrospective sources (leads and context only)

- **PFRA, *The Coffin Corner* 26:6 (2004), Mark Speck, "Interim Coaches: The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly."** Full text read. https://profootballresearchers.com/archives/Website_Files/Coffin_Corner/26-06-1047.pdf. Coryell is listed under "THE GOOD" interim coaches, but the article defines "interim" by midseason timing (quoted above). Coryell entry: "Head Coach Tommy Prothro resigned, and on September 25, Don Coryell was named to replace him."
- **Gene Klein & David Fisher, *First Down and a Billion* (Morrow, 1987), pp. 135-136.** This is the owner's memoir, cited by Wikipedia for Klein negotiating Coryell's release for a 3rd-round pick. Not read. It is the best non-press primary source to pull for contract terms.
- **boltsfromtheblue.com, "A History of the San Diego Chargers: Part 14" (2013-07-28).** Snippet only (403). https://www.boltsfromtheblue.com/2013/7/28/4546826/a-history-of-the-san-diego-chargers-part-14-homecoming. Snippets: "Klein also announced the immediate hiring of Don Coryell as the new head coach." Also: a preliminary deal allowed "the Chargers [to] hire Coryell at the end of the 1978 season in exchange for the team's 1980 3rd-round draft pick", and "Klein contacted Coryell that Sunday evening and offered Coryell the job immediately."
- **Tom Shanahan, tomshanahan.report (2023-07).** Full text read. Coryell's first team meeting was September 25, 1978: "Then, Coryell entered the same room to meet his new players." There is nothing on contract or interim status.
- **SI 2010 obituary "Don Coryell 1924-2010"** (search snippet) says Klein forced Prothro's resignation. **Wikipedia** (lead only): the 1978 season infobox says "(interim)", but the Don Coryell article describes a permanent hire. Wikipedia is internally inconsistent.

### Verdict: PERMANENT AT HIRE, moderate confidence

No contemporaneous snippet uses interim language. The Post's same-day headline is "Coryell Hired" and its subhead says he "Replaces Prothro As Coach". Most tellingly, the Chargers paid draft-pick compensation (a 3rd-rounder) to St. Louis to free his contract. A team does not pay a pick for a 12-game caretaker. Retrospective accounts (team history and Klein's memoir) say Klein had pre-arranged to make Coryell his next coach. Coryell was also an established five-year NFL head coach, not a promoted assistant. Confidence is moderate, not high, because no full contemporaneous article could be read and no contract length at hire was found. The NYT 1978-09-26 story, LA Times C3 and Klein's memoir pp. 135-136 should settle it.

**Quotes (B1, full text; word-for-word extracts):**

- "He was given a multi-year pact by the team."
- "Neither length nor salary were disclosed."
- "Berry was offered the job Wednesday night and Meyer was informed Thursday morning by Sullivan of the ouster."
- Berry "rehired Rust as his first move in his first job as an NFL head coach."
- GM Patrick Sullivan: "Yesterday was an extremely difficult day, but I feel we have come out stronger."
- Neither "interim" nor "permanent" appears in the article.

**B2 (snippet only):** the search index summary says Berry "has a contract that runs through 1987" and was "hired 12 hours before Sullivan told Meyer he was fired." The exact sentence could not be retrieved; verify in the SI Vault.

**Contract terms at hire:** a multi-year contract (B1), with length and salary undisclosed. SI reportedly gave the expiry as 1987 (B2, unverified wording). That would be the 1984 remainder plus three full seasons.

**End-of-season coaching search or new contract after 1984:** none found. A later extension exists but is not a retention-after-interim decision. *The Christian Science Monitor*, 1986-09-03, Ross Atkin, "A thoughtful voice in pro football wars" (full text, https://www.csmonitor.com/1986/0903/rberry.html), says: "He has signed a new five-year contract." That came after his first full season and the Super Bowl XX appearance. It matches UPI, 1990-02-26 (full text, https://www.upi.com/Archives/1990/02/26/Patriots-fire-head-coach-Raymond-Berry/9043636008400/): "Berry ... had one year left on his contract with New England and the buyout price was $550,000."

**The "stable man for the moment" quote:** it surfaced only in a retrospective source, the Boston Globe obituary by John Powers (2026-06-01, https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/01/sports/raymond-berry-patriots-obituary/, text read): "'We feel we've hired a stable man for the moment,' said general manager Patrick Sullivan." The obituary does not give the original 1984 source or date, and it does not call Berry interim. "For the moment" reads as a remark about stabilizing a turbulent team. It is not a statement of contract status, and it sits against a contemporaneous report of a multi-year deal.

### Retrospective sources (context only)

- *The Christian Science Monitor*, 1986-01-13, Ross Atkin, "The coaching life -- and four who made it to the playoffs" (full text, https://www.csmonitor.com/1986/0113/pco.html): Berry was named Coach of the Year "in his first full season with the Patriots". No status detail.
- UPI, 1986-01-10, "Experience carries a lot of weight in the playoffs" (full text, https://www.upi.com/Archives/1986/01/10/Experience-carries-a-lot-of-weight-in-the-playoffs/5474505717200/): "Berry replaced Meyer." No status detail.
- PFRA *Coffin Corner* 2004 (full text): lists Berry under timing-based "interim coaches": "Sullivan named Hall of Fame receiver Raymond Berry as his new coach, who immediately rehired Rust." It attributes the firing to owner Billy Sullivan, whereas UPI B1 attributes it to GM Patrick Sullivan.
- Wikipedia's 1984 season infobox says "(interim)". The Raymond Berry article does not. Its supporting cite is Chicago Tribune 1985-12-20, Jim Donaldson, "Berry Again Overcomes The Skeptics" (blocked domain, not read).

### Verdict: PERMANENT AT HIRE, high confidence

The wire report on the day after the hire states that he "was given a multi-year pact by the team". That is the decisive marker the study specified. SI's issue the next week reportedly put the contract through 1987. No contemporaneous source found uses interim language. No post-season search or separate post-season retention contract appears in any source found. The 1986 five-year deal is a later extension, not a conversion from interim. The GM's "stable man for the moment" line is reported only retrospectively and does not outweigh a contemporaneously reported multi-year contract.

- L4 (full text, word-for-word): "Levy, who guided the Bills to a 2-5 record after replacing fired Hank Bullough, has plenty to work with on offense." Also: "Bills Coach Marv Levy plays down the schedule."
- L1 (snippet, paraphrase-grade): Levy "promised to bring 'a sense o[f] organization'" to a team on its third head coach in 13 months. - **Wikipedia** (leads only): the Marv Levy article says he was "initially hired as a television analyst" and replaced Bullough "nine games into the regular season as head coach". The 1986 season article says the Bills "hired former Kansas City coach Marv Levy to replace him". Neither says "interim".

### Verdict: UNRESOLVED, leaning PERMANENT AT HIRE, low-to-moderate confidence

No contemporaneous source found calls Levy interim. Every period wire item (AP 11/4, UPI 11/9, UPI 1987) describes him as the Bills' coach who replaced the fired Bullough. The owner's own retrospective account frames him as Wilson's deliberate choice, and he came from outside the organization. None of that is consistent with a caretaker appointment. The decisive item, though, is contract terms at hire, and it could not be confirmed. The New York Daily News page of 1986-11-04 appears (in an unverified index summary) to report that Levy received a multi-year contract. If that sentence is confirmed by reading the page, the verdict moves to "permanent at hire, high confidence". The NYT and AP stories of 1986-11-04 should also carry the contract terms. Until then this one stays formally unresolved.
