## Midseason hires (internal-hire coding question)
Don Coryell (San Diego Chargers, 1978): (1) Hired Monday 1978-09-25 by Chargers owner Gene Klein, the same day Tommy Prothro resigned (team 1-3 per Wikipedia; one source says 1-4). Klein called Coryell that morning and he accepted; Klein then negotiated his release from the Cardinals for a third-round pick (sources conflict on the draft year, 1980 vs 1981). (2) No contemporaneous wire text was retrievable. A 1978 news index headline reads "Prothro Retires Early As Coach Of Chargers". The Wikipedia 1978 Chargers season page labels Coryell "interim" (8-4), but the Wikipedia Don Coryell article and the Bolts From the Blue history describe an immediate hire as the new head coach with no interim language, and he coached through 1986. Best reading: hired as head coach, not an announced interim; the "interim" label looks like a Wikipedia cataloging convention for midseason replacements. Moderate confidence. (3) No prior role with the Chargers. He was St. Louis Cardinals head coach through 1977 (dismissed by owner Bill Bidwill on 1978-02-10), then out of football in San Diego until the hire. Raymond Berry (New England Patriots, 1984): (1) Meyer was fired Thursday 1984-10-25 at 5-3 by Patriots management (Wikipedia names no decision maker; SI reported Berry was hired about 12 hours before Meyer was told; owner Billy Sullivan, GM Patrick Sullivan). Berry's first game was 1984-10-28 (30-20 win over the Jets); he went 4-4, team 9-7. (2) Contemporaneous-style wording found: GM Patrick Sullivan, "We feel we've hired a stable man for the moment" (quoted in the 2026 Boston Globe obituary; the 1984 original was not seen). Wikipedia's 1984 season page lists him as "interim head coach", but no retrieved contemporaneous source uses the word interim; the CSM 1986 profile does not say interim; he signed a new five-year contract in 1986 and coached through 1989. Status at hire is therefore ambiguous ("for the moment" suggests provisional); flag for the reviewer (needs a 1984 newspaper source). (3) Not a Patriots employee immediately before the hire. He was Patriots receivers coach 1978-81 (hired by Chuck Fairbanks, kept by Ron Erhardt, dismissed with the whole staff after the 2-14 1981 season), then out of football in 1982, 1983 and early 1984, working in Medfield, MA (real estate development / a cap-and-hat company, per sources). 1983 role with the organization: none. Marv Levy (Buffalo Bills, 1986): (1) Hired Monday 1986-11-03 by founding owner Ralph C. Wilson Jr., who fired Hank Bullough that day (after a 6-point Week 9 loss to Tampa Bay; AP via the University Daily Kansan reportedly called Levy the third Bills head coach in 13 months, not directly fetched). Levy's first game was 1986-11-09; the Bills went 2-5 under him. (2) Bills.com headlines it "Marv Levy hired as head coach"; no retrieved source says interim and none gives contract terms. Fan wikis list him as "interim" but with no contemporaneous quote (original AP/UPI text not retrieved). He then coached 11 full seasons (through 1997), consistent with a permanent hire. (3) Not on the Bills coaching staff. Per Wikipedia's 1986 Bills season page he had been a television analyst for the team during the 1986 preseason (a broadcast role) and was hired away from an executive role with the Montreal Alouettes (CFL). Earlier he coached the USFL Chicago Blitz in 1984 and was out of coaching in 1985 (search summaries; 1985 not directly verified). So the only tie to the Bills is the 1986 preseason TV role, in the hire year rather than the prior season. Ron Meyer (Indianapolis Colts, 1986): (1) Colts replaced Rod Dowhower after a 17-3 loss to San Diego on 1986-11-30 (team 0-13); Meyer was named head coach on 1986-12-01 (UPI, "Colts name Meyer head coach", University Daily Kansan, 1986-12-02). Owner Robert Irsay; Meyer said he and Irsay reached a "long-term commitment" about 2 a.m. that day. First game 1986-12-07 (28-23 over Atlanta); Colts went 3-0 under him (3-13 overall). (2) Contemporaneous UPI wording: "Colts name Meyer head coach", no interim language, "long-term commitment". Wikipedia's 1986 Colts page labels him interim, which contradicts the UPI report. Best reading: permanent head coach (he coached until fired on 1991-10-01 after an 0-5 start). Fairly confident. (3) No prior Colts role. After being fired by New England in 1984 he was out of coaching (Dallas-based sports agency Athletic Associates; per Wikipedia he had agreed to become Purdue head coach before taking the Colts job). Not with the Colts in 1985. Summary for the internal-hire coding: in all four cases the "role with the organization in the prior season" is produced by the midseason hire itself (the hire year is the season before the anchor year). None was an incumbent staff member promoted from within: Coryell (out of football, ex-Cardinals HC), Berry (ex-Patriots assistant 1978-81, out of football 1982-84, no 1983 role), Levy (outsider; 1986 preseason TV analyst, Montreal executive), Meyer (outsider; ex-Patriots HC fired 1984). The "interim coach retained next year" belief is not supported by what I could retrieve: Wikipedia labels Coryell, Berry and Meyer interim, but contemporaneous reporting I could retrieve describes Meyer as named head coach with a "long-term commitment", Levy and Coryell as hired head coach, and only Berry's status is genuinely ambiguous ("for the moment"). Recommend recoding all four as non-internal (external) hires if the rule is meant to capture prior organizational ties.
