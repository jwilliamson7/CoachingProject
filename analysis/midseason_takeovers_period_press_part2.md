# Midseason takeovers, part 2: insider status and interim vs permanent, from period sources

Compiled 2026-10-28. Covers six midseason NFL head-coach takeovers: Ed Khayat (PHI 1971), Harland Svare (SD 1971), Sid Gillman (HOU 1973), John McVay (NYG 1976), Tommy Hudspeth (DET 1976) and Homer Rice (CIN 1978). Part 1 (Coryell, Berry, Levy) is `analysis/midseason_hires_period_press.md`.

## How to read this file

- **Contemporaneous** means press published at the time of the takeover or by the end of that season. **Period team publication** means an official club media guide or a league/annual reference book. Guides published *before* the takeover (the preseason guide of the same year) are contemporaneous evidence of a man's job at the club before he was promoted. Guides published the *following* preseason are the club's own official record, written within a year of the event; they are treated as strong period evidence but are kept separate from press. **Retrospective** means anything later (books, obituaries, team-history web pages, Wikipedia). Retrospective material is listed only as leads or context, never as evidence of status at hire.
- **Read status:** "full text" means the document's text was fetched and examined. For the archive.org media guides this is the scan's OCR text (`_djvu.txt`), so OCR errors appear in some raw quotes (corrected spellings are given in brackets where needed). "Snippet only" means the page could not be fetched (paywall, 403 or blocked domain), and what is recorded comes from the search engine's index of that page's OCR text. "Index only" means only the page's headline/names index was visible.
- **Quote fidelity caveat (same as part 1):** WebFetch and WebSearch return text through a summarizing model. Quotes marked "full text" were requested word-for-word and are probably exact, but should be spot-checked before they are printed. Snippet-only material is paraphrase-grade even when it appears in quotation marks; attribution of a snippet to a particular newspaper page is also uncertain where noted.
- **What was reachable this time (new since part 1):** archive.org's NFL media guides (full OCR text, via the `archive.org/download/...` redirect to the storage node) turned out to be the best period source and settled most of the insider questions. Google Books' public feed endpoint (`google.com/books/feeds/volumes?q=...`) returns snippets (the `googleapis.com` API returned HTTP 429). The Ann Arbor District Library (aadl.org) and The Harvard Crimson archive returned full text.
- **Not reachable:** nytimes.com (blocked for both search and fetch); newspapers.com page views (HTTP 403; only the search index's OCR snippets are usable); vault.si.com (HTTP 404 to the fetcher; snippets only); digital.bentley.umich.edu (Michigan Daily, 403); newspaper.library.tamu.edu (The Battalion, 403); texashistory.unt.edu (bot-validation wall); idnc.library.illinois.edu and nyshistoricnewspapers.org (403); Google News Archive scans (image only, no text); newspaperarchive.com (blocked for the search tool); web.archive.org (blocked). UPI's online archive starts around 1980 and so does not cover these events.
- **Definitional trap (repeated from part 1):** retrospective works, including PFRA's *Coffin Corner* 26:6 (2004) and Wikipedia staff lists, call any midseason replacement "interim" by timing alone. For this study, "interim" is coded only from period wording or terms (an "interim" label, "for the rest of the season", or a separate post-season rehiring/new contract).

- K1 (full text, before the takeover): title line "ED KHAYAT, Assistant Coach". Bio: "Handling the Eagles' defensive line in his first year of coaching association with the club". It also says he returned to Philadelphia after four years as an assistant to Tom Fears at New Orleans (paraphrase).
- K2 (snippet, paraphrase-grade): Tose told a news conference Wednesday (= 1971-10-06) that Khayat "said he would produce a winner."

- K7 (full text): "Oddly enough, when Philadelphia Owner Leonard Tose said good-by to Williams after three weeks last fall," / "he promoted Khayat to the top job—and retained Fears as an assistant—" / "although Eddie never had been a head coach before and Fears had." - **Interim wording:** none of K1-K8 uses "interim", "acting" or "for the rest of the season". All period sources say he was "named head coach" or "promoted ... to the top job".
- **Contract terms at appointment:** NOT FOUND.
- **Post-season search or new contract after 1971:** NOT FOUND. He coached 1972 and was fired after a 2-11-1 season (K8: "the entire coaching staff was released"; McCormack named January 1973).

### Retrospective sources (leads only)

- Jim Gehman, "Where are they now? DT Ed Khayat", philadelphiaeagles.com, 2021-01-14 (full text, https://www.philadelphiaeagles.com/news/where-are-they-now-dt-ed-khayat): "He then returned to the Eagles a second time in 1971 as an assistant coach." "But after three games, all losses, the team's owner, Leonard Tose, dismissed Head Coach Jerry Williams and promoted Khayat to the top job." Khayat's own recollection: "It was a difficult thing when you're working somewhere and you lose a guy like that and you're asked to, on an interim basis, finish the year." This is the strongest "interim" statement found, but it is a recollection fifty years later.
- *The NFL's Official Encyclopedic History of Professional Football* (NFL Properties, 1977), Google Books snippet: "... Tose fired Wil- liams and promoted defensive line coach Ed Khayat to head ..."
- John Maxymuk, *NFL Head Coaches* (2012), snippet: "Tose and Ed Khayat was hired as interim coach." Fran Zimniuch, *Philadelphia Eagles: Where Have You Gone?* (2015), snippet: "Ed Khayat who had been at New Orleans was promoted to interim head coach."
- PFRA *Coffin Corner* 26:6 (2004), full text: "He was canned in favor of defensive coach Ed Khayat" (listed among timing-defined interim coaches).
- Wikipedia, 1971 Eagles season, staff list: "Head coach – Ed Khayat (Interim, 6-4-1)" (uncited). The Ed Khayat article does not say interim.

### Verdict

- **(a) Insider: YES, high confidence.** He was the Eagles' defensive line coach (title "Assistant Coach") for the 1971 season, hired in the 1971 offseason after four years on the Saints staff. The pre-season 1971 media guide (K1) is direct period proof.
- **(b) Interim or permanent: UNRESOLVED.** Every period source reached (K6-K8, and the Fort Worth snippet) says "named head coach" or "promoted to the top job", with no "interim" label, no "rest of the season" wording and no contract terms. The only "interim" statements are retrospective, the most specific being Khayat's own 2021 recollection that he was asked "on an interim basis, finish the year". No post-season rehiring or new contract was found either way. The Philadelphia papers of 1971-10-06/07 (K4 and the Inquirer) should settle it.
- **(c) Date and decision-maker:** Wednesday 1971-10-06 (K6, K8; K2 places Tose's news conference on Wednesday). Wikipedia's Jerry Williams article dates the firing to 1971-10-05 (lead only). Decision: owner Leonard Tose (K2, K7). GM Pete Retzlaff's role in the decision is not established.

### Retrospective sources (leads only)

### Verdict

- **(a) Insider: YES, high confidence.** Executive Vice-President and General Manager from 1973-03-02, hired by owner Bud Adams (G1, a pre-season document). He also reportedly ran the offense from the fourth game (lead).
- **(b) INTERIM in substance (a rest-of-season stopgap), moderate confidence.** No source uses the word "interim", and as GM he was not a caretaker assistant. But the club's chronology says he took the coaching role "for the rest of year". SI reported at the time that he planned to give up coaching and look for a replacement during the off-season. On 1974-01-18 he announced he would stay on "for at least one more season, ending speculation that a new head coach would be named", after a players' petition. That is a separate post-season retention decision. If the study's coding needs an explicit "interim" label, code this as "rest-of-season appointment, retained after the season".
- **(c) Date and decision-maker:** the team chronology gives 1973-10-16. The NYT story is dated 10-16, which suggests the announcement came on Monday 10-15 (unverified). The Denver loss was on 10-14. Decision: Gillman himself, as GM ("hired himself", G4); owner Bud Adams's part in the decision is not documented in what was read.

### Verdict

- **(a) Insider: YES, high confidence.** He was a Giants assistant coach ("Research and Development") from March 1976 (M1, M7).

Against that, no contract or term beyond 1976 was reported at appointment, his return was still an open question in mid-December (M8), the club then "rehired" him on a new two-year contract (M9), and SI described that contract as narrowly *won* on the late-season results (M11). That is the profile of a trial or interim appointment with a post-season retention decision. The October 26 AP stories (M2-M4) and NYT M9 should settle the wording at appointment.
- **(c) Date and decision-maker:** Monday 1976-10-25 (the day after the 27-0 loss to Pittsburgh on Sunday 10-24; wire stories dated 10-26). Decision: president Wellington Mara and director of operations Andy Robustelli (M7). Retention for 1977-78 was announced 1976-12-14 (NYT 12-15).
