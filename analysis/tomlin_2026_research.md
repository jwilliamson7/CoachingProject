## 3. Evidence for involuntary

1. Tomlin's own words: "I thought it was a good time for the organization" and "we didn't have a lot of success in the playoffs in recent years" (ESPN 2026-04-26). This links the exit to performance and organizational interest, not only personal or family reasons, which contradicts the family-only framing. It is Tomlin's admission, not evidence of coercion.
2. Rooney's "I think we all knew we were getting towards the end" (CBS Pittsburgh 2026-01-14) and "wasn't shocked." Consistent with an organization that had priced in a change; also consistent with a long-tenured coach winding down.
3. Seventh straight playoff loss (30-6), no playoff win since January 2017, "Fire Tomlin" chants in Week 13 and after the loss (ESPN). Schefter (2025-12-06) said the team would "review the full body of work" and had to decide the 2027 option by March 1, so a lame-duck or option-declined outcome was live. Context for pressure; no sourced report says it was applied.
4. Yahoo Sports (2026-01-13) noted Rooney publicly made no effort to talk him out of it and speculated Tomlin was "perhaps one step ahead of someone else determining his fate." [OPINION/speculation]
5. Rooney declined to say whether the playoff losses played a role (secondary summaries). Absence of denial, weak.
6. Rooney said he had expected to talk about 2026, but the Steelers had not, per any report found, extended or publicly committed to Tomlin beyond the option decision; Tomlin gave less assurance of returning than in prior years (ESPN 2026-01-13/14 recap).

No report found from Schefter, Fowler, Rapoport, Pelissero, The Athletic, Post-Gazette, Tribune-Review or Steelers Depot that Rooney or Khan wanted a change, pressured him, or signaled they would not extend him. I could not retrieve the Post-Gazette, Tribune-Review, or Steelers Depot pieces directly (search did not surface them); The Athletic (DeFabo) and Steelers Depot (Carney) appear only as quote attributions inside a Yardbarker aggregation. This is a gap, not a negative finding.

## 4. Evidence for voluntary

1. Pre-exit reporting that the team wanted him: Schefter 2025-12-06 "not going to fire" (ESPN), CBS Pittsburgh same day, SI/Steelers On SI 2025-12-07 (unnamed sources: Rooney and organization do not plan to fire him). The Steelers have not fired a head coach since 1968.
2. Rooney on the record: "It was his decision"; "I was certainly willing to take another run at it with Mike" and had come to the meeting to discuss 2026. The meeting went "in a different direction." This is the party with an incentive to save face, so it is weaker than independent reporting, but it is specific and was consistent across outlets.
3. ESPN independent sourcing (Pryor/Fowler 2026-01-16): decision initiated by Tomlin, possibility since summer 2025, "not made in haste," sources close to Tomlin said fan discontent was not a factor. Tomlin himself framed it as personal ("loneliness with leadership").
4. Contract/rights: Tomlin resigned while under contract, with no reported release or payout. Rapoport (2026-01-17): suitors approached Pittsburgh for what "amounted to a trade" and Tomlin turned them away. ESPN: Steelers retain rights, with possible compensation if he coaches elsewhere before end of 2027. Contract also had a no-trade clause (Schefter). A team that was pushing him out would not ordinarily hold his rights. Caveat: Florio (via Yahoo 2026-08-09) says the 2027 option apparently was not exercised, so rights/compensation are uncertain [UNVERIFIED, secondary]; the March 1 option decision may have been moot after resignation.
5. Subsequent behavior: told teams he would not coach in 2026, took NBC studio analyst job (announced; The Athletic reported first), "never say never" in September, "it's not a 32-team league for me" (selective). Not behavior of a coach who was fired, but also not proof he was not squeezed.
6. Insider Jordan Schultz (X, 2026-01-13): "Mike Tomlin was NOT fired. He's voluntarily leaving the organization, so the Steelers will retain his rights." (single-source tweet; consistent with the above.) Pre-exit report (via SI) also mentioned a TV offer "in his back pocket."
7. Rooney's stated reason: "more of a family-related decision." Category C would rest on this, but it is Rooney's characterization, and Tomlin himself never cited family; he cited loneliness, the playoff record, and the organization's interest.

## 5. Context bearing on pressure

- Playoff record: last playoff win January 2017 (18-16 over KC); lost AFCCG 36-17 to NE that year; seven straight playoff losses through 2026-01-12. Never a losing season in 19 years; 13 playoff appearances; one Super Bowl (XLIII).
- Fans: "Fire Tomlin" chants in Week 13 2025 and after the Houston loss. Sources close to Tomlin said not a factor.
- Ownership/GM: No reporting found of Rooney-Khan-Tomlin friction. Khan knew by noon on 01-13. Rooney said Khan and he would lead the search. Rooney said no current staff member is a candidate; Rodgers (one-year deal) likely affected because he "came here to play for Mike."
- Speculation: SI Steelers On SI 2025-12-07 "divorce might be coming" [OPINION; its own sources said no firing].
- Successor named within two weeks (McCarthy, 01-24/27).

## 6. Cowher comparison (January 2007)

- Cowher called Dan Rooney on 2007-01-04; announced 2007-01-05 after an 8-8 season (one year after Super Bowl XL), with one year left on a roughly $4 million contract; not retiring, wanted time with family (youngest daughter had about 2.5 years of high school left, family had moved to Raleigh). Quote: "Do I looked burned out?" (asked at a recent news conference); Dan Rooney: "History will look back on Bill Cowher as one of the great coaches of all time." (Patriots.com reproduction of the 2007 AP story https://www.patriots.com/news/cowher-resigns-as-steelers-head-coach-98596 ; Picayune Item https://www.picayuneitem.com/?p=48964)
- Contract friction was reported contemporaneously: extension talks the previous summer never got past preliminary stages; ESPN said Cowher wanted pay among the league's top (perhaps double his salary); the Steelers gave no sign they would pay $8 million; they offered him the chance to return for 2007 and finish his contract, which the report said would probably not satisfy either side. AP: Rooney did not think money was the sticking point. A 2007 report said money was believed to have played a major role even though Cowher insisted it did not. Steelers held his rights for 2007 (any suitor would owe compensation).
- Later: no reporting found that he was forced out. In a later podcast he said he "couldn't come up to the grips of coaching another team... This was my team." (Baker City Herald summary.) He joined CBS in 2007 and has not coached since.
- Same-standard comparison: both resigned under contract, both teams retained rights, both stated family reasons, neither team fired. Differences: Cowher's was a dispute over pay/extension (a contract-driven exit, which the study's protocol would arguably treat as leaving because the team would not meet terms, not a firing), the team offered to keep him for 2007, and he is much closer to a documented family/non-football reason (Category C) with a contemporaneous extension stalemate. Tomlin's case has no reported pay dispute but has an explicit performance-linked statement from Tomlin and a year-after-year playoff slump. Applying the protocol identically: Cowher has contemporaneous family reasoning plus a retained-and-offered-return posture, which is the weakest-possible involuntary case; Tomlin has the same posture but with Tomlin himself conceding the organization's interest and the playoff failures.

## 7. Recommendation

Label: VOLUNTARY, Category C (documented family/other non-football reasons, with a contemporaneous "team wanted him back / not firing" record), coded as a competing-event (non-firing exit), not a firing. Confidence: moderate, about 70 percent.

Reasoning: under the protocol the default is involuntary absent positive, contemporaneous evidence of leaving on his own terms while still wanted. Here that evidence exists and comes from two directions: pre-exit insider reporting (ESPN 2025-12-06) that the team would not fire him, and post-exit ESPN reporting from sources close to Tomlin that the decision was his, long contemplated (since summer 2025), and not driven by fan pressure; plus Rooney's on-record account that he planned to keep him and the retained-rights/trade-inquiry facts. I found no documented report of pressure, an ultimatum, a buyout, or a release. The suspicion that it was face-saving rests on inference (the playoff slump, "getting towards the end," Tomlin's "good time for the organization"), not on reporting. Caution for category: Tomlin's own explanation emphasizes loneliness and the playoff record rather than family, so Category C rests on Rooney's characterization and "personal" reasons; Category B (retired while successful and retained) does not fit cleanly because he did not retire. If the author prefers to avoid a Category C call, label it "voluntary, other (stepped down while retained)".

Single piece of evidence that would change it to involuntary: a contemporaneous report from a credible insider or beat writer (for example The Athletic or Post-Gazette) that Rooney or Khan told Tomlin before 2026-01-13 that he would not be extended, that the option would be declined, or that his job was at risk, or that Pittsburgh paid him out or released him from the contract.

Gaps: Post-Gazette, Tribune-Review, Steelers Depot and The Athletic originals were not retrieved; the Complex and a few outlet summaries were relayed by the fetch tool rather than verbatim; contract/rights status after March 1, 2026 is contested (ESPN says rights retained through 2027; Florio/Yahoo says the option may not have been exercised, Athlon headline says no draft compensation).
