==========================================================

Scope: all 219 rows of analysis/event_labels_final.csv with reason == "losing_finale"
(label_source == "deterministic", event = 1). Row-level output:
analysis/losing_finale_review.csv. Every row was screened against
Coaches/<name>/all_coaching_history.csv. Web research focused on coaches who never
coached again or were 60+ at exit, plus every coach whose next-season job was a head
job elsewhere. No death within about a year of a last season was found other than
McCafferty.

Screening rule used for the moved_* findings: a row is moved_nfl_hc or
moved_college_or_other_league only when the coach left in order to take the new job
(resigned for it, was released from contract for it, or was traded). A coach who was
fired, bought out or forced out first and was hired elsewhere afterwards is "none";
those rows are listed separately below so the author can see them.

5. VERIFICATION ANSWERS (data fix)
----------------------------------
(a) J.D. Roberts, Saints 1970.
    Fears was fired by owner John Mecom on Nov 3, 1970 (reports dated Nov 4: "Fears
    fired by Saints"), after a 1-5-1 start (7 games). Roberts' job immediately before
    was head coach of the Richmond Roadrunners of the Atlantic Coast Football League
    (1969), renamed the Richmond Saints for 1970. That club was the New Orleans Saints'
    minor-league affiliate. ProFootballArchives lists him as 1970 Richmond coach (1-7)
    before he moved up. He had been a Saints scout and linebackers coach in 1967-68.
    So he was not on the NFL Saints staff when hired (outside the NFL staff), but he
    came from the club's own farm team and was a former Saints assistant. Whether that
    counts as "outside" is a definitional call. Note: the repo history file lists
    Richmond only for 1969.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._D._Roberts
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Roadrunners
    https://profootballarchives.com/coaches/robe04300.html
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_New_Orleans_Saints_season

(b) Charlie Waller, Chargers.
    Insider: Chargers offensive backfield coach from 1966 (repo history 1966-68). He
    took over on Nov 14, 1969 when Gillman stepped down for health reasons, with
    Gillman staying as GM. Record 4-1 in 1969, 5-6-3 in 1970. His stint ended because
    Gillman's health recovered and Gillman resumed the head job. Wikipedia, citing the
    Chargers' history page, says Gillman was named head coach on Dec 30, 1970. The PFRA
    Hall of Fame bio says he "asked to return to the active coaching job" in 1971.
    Waller stayed on staff as offensive coordinator in 1971, then went to Washington
    in 1972. No source calls it a firing. In effect it was a demotion back to
    assistant, ordered by the boss returning to the job, not a performance dismissal
    and not a voluntary exit by Waller. How to code it is the author's call.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Waller_(American_football)
    https://www.profootballresearchers.com/archives/Website_Files/Coffin_Corner/27-03-1080.pdf

(c) Sid Gillman, Chargers 1969.
    Yes, health. Per the PFRA Coffin Corner bio (courtesy of the Pro Football Hall of
    Fame), a duodenal ulcer and a hiatal hernia forced a premature, temporary
    retirement in 1969. Wikipedia cites the hiatal hernia. He stepped down
    after nine games with a 4-5 record and stayed on as general manager. (His 1971
    exit, 4-6 after 10 games, followed a clash with ownership. The PFRA bio and other
    sources do not give health as the reason, and he coached for Dallas in 1972.)
    https://www.profootballresearchers.com/archives/Website_Files/Coffin_Corner/27-03-1080.pdf
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_San_Diego_Chargers_season

(d) Tom Fears. Confirmed: 1970 was his last season as Saints coach. He was fired
    seven games in (Nov 3, 1970) and was Eagles offensive coordinator in 1971-72 (repo
    history). He never coached the Saints again.


6. AMBIGUOUS OR WORTH A SECOND LOOK (all kept at event 1 unless listed in section 2)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bud Grant 1985: see section 2. The author expected a pre-announcement; the dates
  say otherwise.
- John McKay 1984: B per the pre-announcement rule. Health was also cited, so C is
  an alternative. The recode holds either way.
- Rich Kotite NYJ 1996: announced Fri Dec 20, 1996, two days before the finale, that
  he was stepping down at 1-14. That meets the letter of "announced before the final
  game", but it was a record-driven resignation ("Kotite fires himself"), not a
  retirement. Not proposed.
- Bum Phillips NO 1985 (age 62): quit Nov 25, 1985 with four games left, effective
  immediately, under new owner Tom Benson. Never coached again. No advance
  announcement and no health reason. Not proposed.
- Marion Campbell ATL 1989 (age 60): out Nov 28, 1989 at 3-9. One source lists him as
  fired and another as retired. No advance announcement found.
- Dick Vermeil PHI 1982: resigned Jan 10, 1983, after the season, citing burnout.
  Post-season, so it stays a firing, unless the author reads burnout as a health
  exit (category C).
- Post-season retirements or resignations that stay firings under the protocol: Levy
  1997 (Dec 31, 1997, age 72), Coughlin 2015, Spurrier 2003, Switzer 1997,
  Schottenheimer 1998, Flores 1987, Belichick 2023 (mutual parting).
- Dick MacPherson NE 1992: missed seven games after intestinal surgery, but was fired
  Jan 8, 1993. A documented firing, not a health exit.
- Gene Stallings PHO 1989: announced in November 1989 that he would not return (to
  leave, not to retire) and was removed at once. Kept as a firing.
- Ray Perkins NYG 1982: a pre-announced departure (Dec 15, 1982), but for a college
  job, so it falls under the quasi-firing rule.
